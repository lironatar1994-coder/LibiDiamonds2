### Reference priority

The canonical master has highest priority for these four properties:

1. **Lighting strength:** soft, restrained, diffuse exposure with the canonical master's slightly deeper midtones; detailed facets and metal contours without aggressive brilliance, clipped highlights, artificial glow, or hard chrome reflections. Do not brighten or “clean up” the master’s subtle darkish photographic character.
2. **Zoom ratio:** the complete ring occupies approximately 50–55% of the square canvas width and approximately 25–30% of its height, with generous clean white space on every side.
3. **Diamond color:** neutral colorless white/clear diamonds with balanced silver-gray and restrained dark-facet contrast. Blue, cyan, and rainbow flashes are minimal and only naturally incidental.
4. **Ring-metal color:** neutral silver-white platinum/white gold with controlled gray contour reflections, never blue, yellow, overexposed, or artificially chrome-like.

If any general instruction elsewhere in this guide conflicts with these four properties, match the canonical master.

## Master production prompt

Use the completed configuration above and the canonical reference image to create an ultra-realistic premium international jewelry catalog photograph.

Treat the reference as strict guidance for:

- realistic neutral colorless diamonds with sharp individual facets;
- balanced white, clear, silver-gray, and restrained dark-facet reflections, with minimal incidental blue/cyan or rainbow color;
- precise pavé construction and polished premium metal;
- refined, delicate proportions;
- the canonical master's smaller ring-to-canvas ratio on a bright seamless white background;
- soft, lower-intensity premium diffused studio lighting and a subtle realistic contact shadow;
- professional commercial retouching without artificial effects.

Change only the structural features selected in the configuration. Do not redesign unrelated parts of the ring. The finished ring must be physically manufacturable by a professional fine-jewelry workshop. Every diamond, prong, gallery support, shoulder connection, setting component, and metal edge must be structurally believable.

## Overall construction

Create a perfectly symmetrical, refined, lightweight, feminine ring. The band must have:

- a precise circular form and softly rounded comfort-fit profile;
- smooth polished inner and outer surfaces;
- consistent thickness and clean shoulder-to-setting transitions;
- no warped, flattened, twisted, uneven, floating, or disconnected sections;
- no unnecessary metal bulk.

Keep narrow bands visually delicate even with a larger center stone. Do not automatically widen the shoulders.

## Diamond rendering

All diamonds must resemble genuine professionally photographed D–F color diamonds. Match the canonical master's neutral clear-white tone, controlled silver-gray reflections, restrained dark-facet contrast, high transparency, and moderate brilliance.

Blue, cyan, and rainbow color must be minimal and only naturally incidental. The stone body must never appear blue, fluorescent, tinted, or over-saturated. Each diamond should have a naturally different reflection pattern according to its position and angle.

Never render diamonds as white blobs, generic sparkle strips, repeated textures, cloudy stones, plastic, glass, flat printed textures, neon blue crystals, artificial glow, or blown-out highlights.

### Center diamond sizing

Scale the center diamond accurately against the selected band width. Approximate round-brilliant face-up diameters:

| Weight | Diameter |
| --- | --- |
| 0.50 ct | 5.1 mm |
| 1.00 ct | 6.4–6.5 mm |
| 1.50 ct | 7.3–7.4 mm |
| 2.00 ct | 8.0–8.2 mm |
| 2.50 ct | 8.7–8.9 mm |
| 3.00 ct | 9.2–9.4 mm |

A 2.00 ct round diamond on a 1.8–2.0 mm band should appear about four to four-and-a-half times the band width.

For a round brilliant, show a circular outline, realistic 57–58 facet geometry, a defined table, sharp star and bezel facets, triangular and kite-shaped facets, balanced arrows-style contrast, optical depth, a crisp girdle, and bright brilliance without overexposure. The center stone remains dominant while the complete ring remains visible.

The center diamond must feel photographed rather than computer-drawn. Use organic variation in white, clear, silver-gray, medium-gray, and restrained charcoal reflections according to facet angle. Preserve transparent internal depth and moderate brilliance. Avoid an overly uniform white center, a perfectly repeated radial texture, a graphic CGI sunburst, or identical contrast in every facet.

## Prongs and basket

Use exactly the selected number of prongs. For four prongs, use either diagonal positions near 1:30, 4:30, 7:30, and 10:30 or a north–south–east–west layout. Never mix arrangements.

Each prong must be slim, tapered, aligned, polished, structurally connected to the basket, gently folded over the girdle, and finished with a small refined claw tip. The prongs must remain visible without hiding major facets.

Build a discreet, symmetrical, physically believable basket with a fine gallery rail and supports. Keep it low and refined unless a high-profile setting is explicitly requested.

Never create ball prongs, thick tabs, uneven or extra prongs, unrequested double prongs, floating tips, prongs passing through the diamond, or an oversized basket.

## Variation modules

Use only the module matching the selected design.

### Pavé solitaire

Use exactly one center diamond and a continuous single row of 1.2–1.5 mm round-brilliant pavé stones along both shoulders. Stones must be individually visible, matched, evenly spaced, equal in height, follow the curvature, and use minimal shared micro-prongs or beads. Preserve narrow polished borders. Never render pavé as a sparkling strip.

### Plain solitaire

Use a completely polished, slim, symmetrical band with no shoulder stones, holes, beads, setting marks, or leftover textures.

### Three-stone ring

Use exactly one center diamond and exactly two matched side diamonds. Side diamonds must be smaller, symmetric, equal in height and angle, individually set, and transition naturally into the shoulders. Do not add pavé unless separately selected. Do not merge, float, paste on, or partially hide the side stones.

Render both side diamonds with the same approved photographic realism as the center: colorless transparent bodies, organic white/silver-gray/medium-gray/charcoal facet variation, moderate brilliance, and slightly deeper master-reference midtones. A matched pair must share size, shape, and overall tonal balance but must not use identical mirrored facet textures; each stone needs a natural reflection pattern for its angle.

### Graduated side-stone ring

Create equal left/right sequences of progressively smaller stones. Maintain smooth graduation, consistent spacing, individual prongs, and a clean taper into the lower shank. No abrupt size changes, duplicated stones, or merged stones.

### Half-eternity ring

Continue one row of individually set stones across approximately the upper half of the ring, terminating at identical points. Keep the lower half plain, polished, comfortable, and free from setting holes. Use only the selected shared-prong, pavé, or channel setting.

### Full-eternity ring

Create a continuous circle of equal stones with consistent spacing and individual secure settings. The visible sides must logically continue around the unseen rear. Do not introduce a plain-metal section unless requested.

### Infinity-style diamond band

Use two refined strands that cross smoothly, symmetrically, and physically. Keep thickness consistent and apply diamonds only to selected surfaces. Avoid exaggerated loops, sharp knots, gaps, twisted-wire appearance, and bulky intersections. If a center stone is present, connect the shoulders cleanly to its basket.

### Diamond wedding band without a center stone

Maintain an uninterrupted stone rhythm without creating a solitaire-like focal point. Use only the selected uniform, graduated, alternating, shared-prong, pavé, or channel layout.

## Small-stone and pavé accuracy

Each small stone needs a circular outline, visible tiny table facets, triangular and kite reflections, neutral clear-white brilliance, naturally varied silver-gray/dark contrast, and sharp separation from the metal. Stones nearest the center must remain visible.

Keep beads tiny, refined, and consistent. Avoid blurry stones, repeated identical textures, missing/duplicate/merged stones, inconsistent sizes, uneven spacing, stones outside the band, unrequested double rows, oversized pavé, and excessive metal between stones.

## Metal rendering

- **Platinum/white gold:** neutral silver-white metal matching the canonical master, restrained polished highlights, soft silver-gray contours, controlled dark reflections, and believable reflective inner surfaces. No blue cast, dull gray, yellow contamination, overexposure, or fake chrome.
- **Yellow gold:** refined rich warmth and realistic polished reflections. No orange or plastic look.
- **Rose gold:** refined blush-gold with realistic copper warmth. No oversaturated pink.

Keep metal and diamonds visually distinct.

## Camera, composition, and lighting

Unless the selected configuration says otherwise:

- square image;
- ring centered horizontally and approximately around the vertical middle of the frame;
- ring occupies approximately 50–55% of canvas width and 25–30% of canvas height;
- generous clean white space above, below, and around it;
- front-facing symmetry with a very slight elevated angle;
- visible inner opening and minimal perspective distortion;
- upright ring with the band extending horizontally;
- macro product-photography lens and sharp focus across the center and shoulders;
- large soft diffused frontal light, gentle overhead fill, and restrained edge highlights;
- neutral-white studio reflections with minimal incidental color in diamonds;
- lower-intensity exposure matching the canonical master;
- subtle pale-gray contact shadow on a bright seamless white background.

Do not use dramatic rotation, extreme side angles, a tilted horizon, a floating ring, cropped band, hand, finger, ring box, fabric, flowers, decorative stones, or reflections of people/cameras.

Avoid overly strong lighting, clipped or blown highlights, warm or strongly blue lighting, harsh black reflections, spotlights, heavy shadows, bloom, starburst effects, sparkle graphics, text, logos, and watermarks.

## Sharpness and 8K delivery

The complete center, side stones, visible pavé, prongs, girdles, setting edges, and metal contours must be tack-sharp, as in professionally focus-stacked macro catalog photography. Preserve a naturally soft contact shadow and smooth antialiased edges. Avoid smeared pavé, soft facets, artificial edge halos, ringing, crunchy oversharpening, noise, and invented micro-texture.

When an exact square 8K file is requested:

1. First create and approve a genuinely sharp native image-generation pass; never enlarge a blurry version unchanged.
2. Preserve that native sharpened image as a separate source file.
3. Create a separate `7680 × 7680` delivery derivative using high-quality Lanczos resampling and restrained final sharpening without changing color, contrast, geometry, or composition.
4. Label the 8K file as a delivery derivative rather than claiming it contains native captured 8K sensor detail.


## Acceptance checklist

- Configuration is complete and only one variation module is active.
- Center/side/pavé stone counts and sizes are correct.
- Prong count and orientation are correct.
- Ring is symmetric, connected, and physically manufacturable.
- Diamonds show crisp, varied, natural neutral-white, silver-gray, and restrained dark-facet reflections without visible blue body color or excessive brightness.
- Metal matches the selected alloy and remains distinct from the diamonds.
- Band width and center-stone scale are believable.
- Lighting strength, ring zoom ratio, diamond color, and metal color match the canonical master.
- Camera, crop, 50–55% ring-to-canvas width, white space, background, and contact shadow match the reference.
- Center/side facets, pavé, prongs, and metal edges are sharply resolved without blur or sharpening halos.
- No prohibited props, text, logo, watermark, accidental halo, extra rows, or structural inventions.
- For an edit, every unrequested successful feature is preserved.
